AI governance review sprint

Best fit: AI inventory, accountability, evaluation evidence, release criteria, transparency duties or ongoing monitoring are unclear.

Work: structure AI use cases against NIST AI RMF / NIST AI 600-1 risk actions, human oversight, evaluation evidence, Article 50 transparency where applicable, release gates and post-deployment change review.

Outputs: bounded AI register, prioritized gaps, review criteria, owner map and decision memo.

AI vendor & supplier assurance

Best fit: SaaS, LLM or external AI suppliers create uncertainty around data use, subprocessors, assurance evidence, fourth-party dependencies or treatment ownership.

Work: structure vendor intake, evidence states, processor obligations, dependency context and risk treatment around named reviewers.

Outputs: reviewed supplier register, targeted evidence requests, treatment decisions and remediation owners.

Continuous assurance review

Best fit: audit evidence, exceptions, issues and retest ownership drift between review cycles.

Work: review evidence freshness, control-test state, open findings, risk acceptance and remediation status on an agreed cadence.

Outputs: refreshed register, exception agenda, retest owners and management summary.

Typical compact sprint

Where the evidence is available at kickoff, a small review can often follow a seven-working-day pattern: scope and evidence requests, assessment and treatment options, then decision memo and handover. The actual delivery date is agreed only after the review boundary and inputs are confirmed.

What we agree first

Scope, evidence access, review period, decision owners, required outputs and acceptance criteria. Timing and commercial terms depend on that brief. Legal judgments, audit opinions and client authorization stay with the responsible reviewers.
